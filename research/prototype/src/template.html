Signal-Originarabica buying desk

Buy early, buy steady, or buy late?

One call each week for a roastery’s next three months of arabica buying. It comes from a fixed rule with two questions: one about the Pacific Ocean, one about the Brazilian summer.

Research signal · not confirmed on new data

What the call means for your orders

The call moves your forward cover, not your volume. Steady buying keeps about 1.5 months of need on order. “Buy early” extends cover to 3 months. “Buy late” lets cover run down so that you buy each lot as late as your operation allows.

Call to plan with

Futures part of the price only. Differentials, freight, storage and financing are not in these amounts.

How the call is made

The rule asks two questions once a year and holds the answer until the next February. It has no fitted weights, so you can check it by hand.

1 · Was the tropical Pacific warm in January?

Sea-surface temperature in the Niño 3.4 region against its normal (NOAA). A warm January is the El Niño side. The rule reads the value from 15 February.

2 · Was the Brazilian summer hotter than usual?

Mean temperature in January–March, when the beans fill, in six regions with 94% of Brazil’s arabica, against the mean of the last five years (NASA). Weather is used 10 days late.

Both yes → buy earlyWarm ocean and hot summer. In 2009–2022, prices rose more often in the months after.
One yes, one no → steadyThe two readings disagree. The rule makes no call.
Both no → buy lateCool ocean and cool summer. In 2009–2022, prices fell more often in the months after.

How far to trust the call

Score = saving against steady buying for each unit of its year-to-year swing. Steady buying scores zero. The ranges are 95% bootstrap ranges.

Use the call as one input, next to your own view of the market.
